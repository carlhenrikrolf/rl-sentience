# Crossmodality

For a pair of a pleasurable feeling and an uncomfortable feeling (p,u), can there be a point at which p feels as pleasurable as u feels uncomfortable?

To make things concrete, consider the following experiment. You have one hand in cold water of temperature c in range C. You use the other hand to drink a beverage with an amount of sugar s in range S. All temperatures in C are uncomfortable and all amounts of sugar in S are pleasurable. The experimenter fixes c and instucts you the participant to vary s by choosing between different beverages with different sweetness labels. You should pick s such that p(s) feels as pleasurable as u(c) feels uncomfortable. Can you do this, or is the question ill-posed?

To clarify what the question is not about. The question is not about whether there is a point where prefering s over c flips to prefering c over s. Prefering (or wanting) is related to decisionmaking (the economics definition), whereas this question is about felt experiences (liking) regardless of whether there is a decision to be made. If there is a point at which p(s) is comparable to u(c), then it would make sense for that to also be the switching point. (Whether that actually holds would be another experiment.) The reverse need not be true, just because there is a switching point does not imply that there need to be a point at which p(s) is comparable to u(c).
